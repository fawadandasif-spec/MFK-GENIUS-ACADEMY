Work & Energy

Learn work, power, energy, kinetic energy, potential energy, conservation of energy and important numerical problems.

1. What is Work?

Work is said to be done when a force causes an object to move through a distance in the direction of the force.

W = F × d

Where:
W = Work
F = Force
d = Displacement

The SI unit of work is Joule (J).

2. Conditions for Work

For mechanical work to be done:

  • A force must act on the object.
  • The object must undergo displacement.
  • The displacement should have a component in the direction of force.

3. Positive and Negative Work

Positive work: Work is positive when force and displacement are in the same direction.

Negative work: Work is negative when force and displacement are in opposite directions.

Same Direction → Positive Work

Opposite Direction → Negative Work

4. What is Energy?

Energy is the capacity of a body to do work.

Energy is a scalar quantity and its SI unit is Joule (J).

5. Forms of Energy

  • Kinetic Energy
  • Potential Energy
  • Thermal Energy
  • Chemical Energy
  • Electrical Energy
  • Light Energy
  • Nuclear Energy

6. Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by a body due to its motion.

KE = ½mv²

Where:
m = mass
v = velocity

Example

A 2 kg object moves with a velocity of 4 m/s. Find its kinetic energy.

KE = ½ × 2 × 4²

KE = 16 J

7. Potential Energy

Potential energy is the energy possessed by a body due to its position or configuration.

Gravitational potential energy is given by:

PE = mgh

Where:
m = mass
g = acceleration due to gravity
h = height

8. Mechanical Energy

Mechanical energy is the sum of kinetic energy and potential energy.

Mechanical Energy = KE + PE

9. Power

Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

P = W ÷ t

Where:
P = Power
W = Work
t = Time

The SI unit of power is Watt (W).

10. Efficiency

Efficiency is the ratio of useful output energy to the total input energy.

Efficiency = (Useful Output ÷ Total Input) × 100%

11. Law of Conservation of Energy

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form to another.

Example

When a ball falls from a height, its gravitational potential energy is gradually converted into kinetic energy.

12. Work-Energy Principle

The work done by the net force on an object is equal to the change in its kinetic energy.

W = ΔKE

13. Numerical Example

Question

A force of 50 N moves an object through a distance of 4 m in the direction of the force. Find the work done.

Given:

F = 50 N
d = 4 m

Formula:

W = F × d

Calculation:

W = 50 × 4

W = 200 J

Answer: Work done = 200 J

14. Quick Revision

  • Work = Force × Displacement.
  • SI unit of work = Joule (J).
  • Energy is the capacity to do work.
  • Kinetic Energy = ½mv².
  • Potential Energy = mgh.
  • Mechanical Energy = KE + PE.
  • Power = Work ÷ Time.
  • SI unit of power = Watt (W).
  • Energy cannot be created or destroyed.
  • Work done by net force = Change in kinetic energy.
