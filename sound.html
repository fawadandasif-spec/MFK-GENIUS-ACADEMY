Sound Waves

Learn the production, propagation and characteristics of sound, including frequency, amplitude, pitch, loudness, speed and echo.

1. What is Sound?

Sound is a form of energy produced by the vibration of an object. It travels through a medium in the form of mechanical waves.

Sound requires a material medium such as a solid, liquid or gas for its propagation.

2. Production of Sound

Sound is produced when an object vibrates. These vibrations cause the particles of the surrounding medium to vibrate.

Examples

  • Vibrating strings of a guitar
  • Vocal cords during speaking
  • Vibrating tuning fork
  • Vibrating surface of a drum

3. Propagation of Sound

Sound travels through a medium by producing successive compressions and rarefactions.

  • Compression: Region where particles are close together.
  • Rarefaction: Region where particles are spread farther apart.

4. Sound is a Longitudinal Wave

Sound waves in air are longitudinal waves because the particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of propagation of the wave.

Sound Wave → Longitudinal Wave

5. Frequency of Sound

Frequency is the number of complete vibrations produced per second.

f = Number of vibrations ÷ Time
SI Unit = Hertz (Hz)

6. Amplitude and Loudness

Amplitude is the maximum displacement of particles from their equilibrium position.

The greater the amplitude of a sound wave, the greater its perceived loudness generally is.

Greater Amplitude → Greater Loudness

7. Pitch and Frequency

Pitch is the characteristic of sound that allows us to distinguish between high and low sounds.

Pitch depends mainly on the frequency of the sound.

Higher Frequency → Higher Pitch
Lower Frequency → Lower Pitch

8. Audible Range

The normal human ear can generally hear frequencies from about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.

Type Frequency Range
Infrasound Below 20 Hz
Audible Sound 20 Hz – 20,000 Hz
Ultrasound Above 20,000 Hz

9. Speed of Sound

The speed of sound depends on the medium and temperature. In general, sound travels faster in solids than in liquids and faster in liquids than in gases.

Solids > Liquids > Gases

The approximate speed of sound in air at room temperature is about 343 m/s.

10. Wave Equation for Sound

The speed, frequency and wavelength of a sound wave are related by:

v = fλ

Where:

v = speed of sound
f = frequency
λ = wavelength

11. Echo

An echo is the repetition of a sound caused by reflection of sound waves from a distant surface.

Examples

  • Sound reflected from a mountain
  • Sound reflected from a large building
  • Sound reflected from a large wall

12. Applications of Ultrasound

Ultrasound consists of sound waves with frequencies above the human audible range.

  • Medical imaging
  • Industrial testing
  • Cleaning delicate objects
  • Detection of internal defects

13. Solved Numerical

Question

A sound wave has a frequency of 500 Hz and a wavelength of 0.68 m. Find its speed.

Given:

f = 500 Hz
λ = 0.68 m

Formula:

v = fλ

Calculation:

v = 500 × 0.68

v = 340 m/s

Answer: Speed of sound = 340 m/s

14. Quick Revision

  • Sound is produced by vibrating objects.
  • Sound is a mechanical wave.
  • Sound requires a material medium.
  • Sound waves in air are longitudinal.
  • Longitudinal waves contain compressions and rarefactions.
  • Frequency determines pitch.
  • Amplitude is related to loudness.
  • Human audible range is approximately 20 Hz–20 kHz.
  • Below 20 Hz = Infrasound.
  • Above 20 kHz = Ultrasound.
  • Wave equation: v = fλ
  • Echo is produced by reflection of sound.
