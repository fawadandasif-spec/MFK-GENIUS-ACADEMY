Wave Motion

Understand wave motion, amplitude, wavelength, frequency, time period, wave speed and types of waves.

1. What is Wave Motion?

Wave motion is the propagation of a disturbance from one point to another through a medium or space, transferring energy without the permanent transfer of matter.

In mechanical waves, particles of the medium oscillate about their mean positions while the disturbance travels through the medium.

2. Important Terms of a Wave

  • Amplitude: Maximum displacement of a particle from its equilibrium position.
  • Wavelength (λ): Distance between two consecutive points in the same phase.
  • Frequency (f): Number of complete vibrations per second.
  • Time Period (T): Time required for one complete vibration.
  • Wave Speed (v): Distance travelled by the wave per unit time.

3. Amplitude

Amplitude is the maximum displacement of a vibrating particle from its mean or equilibrium position.

The amplitude is usually represented by A. Its SI unit is metre (m).

Important Point

For many waves, increasing the amplitude means increasing the energy carried by the wave.

4. Wavelength

Wavelength is the distance between two consecutive points of a wave that are in the same phase.

It is represented by the Greek letter λ (lambda).

SI Unit = metre (m)

Examples of One Wavelength

  • Crest to the next crest
  • Trough to the next trough
  • Compression to the next compression

5. Frequency

Frequency is the number of complete vibrations or cycles produced per second.

Frequency is represented by f.

Frequency = Number of vibrations ÷ Time
SI Unit = Hertz (Hz)

6. Time Period

Time period is the time required to complete one complete vibration or cycle.

It is represented by T.

T = 1 ÷ f
f = 1 ÷ T
SI Unit = second (s)

7. Wave Speed

Wave speed is the distance travelled by a wave per unit time.

v = fλ

Where:

v = wave speed
f = frequency
λ = wavelength

SI Unit = m/s

8. Transverse Waves

In a transverse wave, particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of propagation of the wave.

Important Features

  • Particles vibrate perpendicular to wave direction.
  • They have crests and troughs.
  • Waves on a stretched rope are an example.

9. Longitudinal Waves

In a longitudinal wave, particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of propagation of the wave.

Important Features

  • Particles vibrate parallel to wave direction.
  • They contain compressions and rarefactions.
  • Sound waves in air are longitudinal waves.

10. Transverse vs Longitudinal Waves

Transverse Longitudinal
Particles vibrate perpendicular to wave direction. Particles vibrate parallel to wave direction.
Has crests and troughs. Has compressions and rarefactions.
Example: wave on a stretched rope. Example: sound in air.

11. Relationship Between Wave Quantities

The speed, frequency and wavelength of a wave are related by the following equation:

v = fλ

Since:

f = 1 ÷ T

Therefore:

v = λ ÷ T

12. Solved Numerical

Question

A wave has a frequency of 8 Hz and a wavelength of 5 m. Calculate its speed.

Given:

f = 8 Hz
λ = 5 m

Formula:

v = fλ

Calculation:

v = 8 × 5

v = 40 m/s

Answer: Wave speed = 40 m/s

13. Solved Numerical — Time Period

Question

A wave has a frequency of 20 Hz. Find its time period.

Given:

f = 20 Hz

Formula:

T = 1 ÷ f

Calculation:

T = 1 ÷ 20

T = 0.05 s

Answer: Time period = 0.05 s

14. Quick Revision

  • Wave motion transfers energy from one place to another.
  • Amplitude = maximum displacement.
  • Wavelength = distance between corresponding points.
  • Frequency = vibrations per second.
  • Time period = time for one complete vibration.
  • Frequency unit = Hz.
  • Wavelength unit = m.
  • Wave speed unit = m/s.
  • v = fλ
  • T = 1 ÷ f
  • Transverse waves have crests and troughs.
  • Longitudinal waves have compressions and rarefactions.
