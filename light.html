Light Waves

Learn about light waves, their properties, reflection, refraction, dispersion, visible light and important formulas.

1. What is Light?

Light is a form of electromagnetic radiation that can produce the sensation of vision in our eyes.

Unlike sound, light does not require a material medium to travel. It can travel through vacuum.

2. Electromagnetic Nature of Light

Light is an electromagnetic wave. It consists of oscillating electric and magnetic fields.

  • Light can travel through vacuum.
  • Light is a transverse wave.
  • Light travels at a very high speed.
  • Light carries energy from one place to another.

3. Speed of Light

The speed of light in vacuum is approximately:

c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

This is one of the most important constants in physics.

4. Wavelength and Frequency

Wavelength (λ): The distance between two successive corresponding points of a wave, such as crest to crest or trough to trough.

Frequency (f): The number of complete waves produced per second.

c = fλ

Where:

  • c = speed of light
  • f = frequency
  • λ = wavelength

5. Transverse Nature of Light

Light waves are transverse waves because the oscillations of the electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

This is different from sound waves in air, which are longitudinal.

6. Reflection of Light

Reflection is the bouncing back of light into the same medium when it strikes a surface.

Examples:

  • Seeing your image in a mirror
  • Reflection from a polished metal surface
  • Light reflected from calm water
Angle of Incidence = Angle of Reflection

7. Refraction of Light

Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one transparent medium into another because its speed changes.

Examples:

  • A pencil appears bent in water.
  • Swimming pools appear shallower.
  • Lenses use refraction to form images.

8. Dispersion of Light

Dispersion is the splitting of white light into its component colours when it passes through a prism.

The seven main colours are represented by:

VIBGYOR

Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange and Red.

9. Electromagnetic Spectrum

The electromagnetic spectrum contains different types of electromagnetic radiation arranged according to wavelength or frequency.

Radiation General Feature
Radio Waves Longest wavelengths
Microwaves Used in communication and heating
Infrared Associated with thermal radiation
Visible Light Detected by human eyes
Ultraviolet Higher frequency than visible light
X-rays Used for medical imaging
Gamma Rays Very high frequency radiation

10. Visible Light

Visible light is the part of the electromagnetic spectrum that can be detected by the human eye.

It contains different colours, each having a different wavelength and frequency.

Red light has a longer wavelength than violet light, while violet light has a higher frequency than red light.

11. Solved Numerical

Question:

The frequency of light is 6 × 10¹⁴ Hz. Find its wavelength.

Given:

c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

f = 6 × 10¹⁴ Hz

Formula:

c = fλ

Rearranging:

λ = c ÷ f

Solution:

λ = (3 × 10⁸) ÷ (6 × 10¹⁴)

λ = 5 × 10⁻⁷ m

12. Important Points

  • Light is an electromagnetic wave.
  • Light is a transverse wave.
  • Light can travel through vacuum.
  • Speed of light in vacuum is 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
  • Reflection means bouncing back of light.
  • Refraction means bending of light.
  • Dispersion splits white light into colours.
  • VIBGYOR represents the seven visible colours.
  • Light follows the relationship c = fλ.
