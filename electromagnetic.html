Electromagnetic Waves

Understand electromagnetic waves, their properties, electromagnetic spectrum, uses, wavelength, frequency and important formulas.

1. What are Electromagnetic Waves?

Electromagnetic waves are waves produced by oscillating electric and magnetic fields.

These waves can transfer energy from one place to another without requiring a material medium.

Important: Electromagnetic waves can travel through vacuum.

2. Nature of Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves. The electric field and magnetic field oscillate perpendicular to each other and to the direction of wave propagation.

  • They are transverse waves.
  • They carry energy.
  • They can travel through vacuum.
  • They travel at the speed of light in vacuum.

3. Speed of Electromagnetic Waves

In vacuum, all electromagnetic waves travel at approximately the speed of light.

c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

Here c represents the speed of electromagnetic waves in vacuum.

4. Relationship Between Speed, Frequency and Wavelength

The speed of an electromagnetic wave is related to its frequency and wavelength.

c = fλ

Where:

  • c = speed of light
  • f = frequency
  • λ = wavelength

5. Electromagnetic Spectrum

The electromagnetic spectrum is the complete range of electromagnetic radiation arranged according to wavelength and frequency.

From longest wavelength to shortest wavelength, the main regions are:

Radio → Microwave → Infrared → Visible → Ultraviolet → X-rays → Gamma Rays

6. Radio Waves

Radio waves have the longest wavelengths and relatively low frequencies in the electromagnetic spectrum.

Uses:

  • Radio broadcasting
  • Television communication
  • Wireless communication

7. Microwaves

Microwaves have shorter wavelengths and higher frequencies than radio waves.

Uses:

  • Mobile communication
  • Satellite communication
  • Radar systems
  • Microwave ovens

8. Infrared Radiation

Infrared radiation is commonly associated with thermal radiation.

Uses:

  • Remote controls
  • Thermal imaging
  • Heat sensors

9. Visible Light

Visible light is the part of the electromagnetic spectrum that can be detected by the human eye.

Its colours are commonly represented by:

VIBGYOR

Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange and Red.

10. Ultraviolet Radiation

Ultraviolet radiation has a higher frequency and shorter wavelength than visible violet light.

Uses:

  • Sterilization
  • Security markings
  • Some medical applications

11. X-rays

X-rays have very short wavelengths and high frequencies.

Uses:

  • Medical imaging
  • Checking bones
  • Security scanning

12. Gamma Rays

Gamma rays have extremely high frequencies and very short wavelengths.

Uses:

  • Cancer treatment
  • Sterilization of medical equipment
  • Scientific research

13. Electromagnetic Spectrum Table

Wave Wavelength Example / Use
Radio Waves Longest Radio communication
Microwaves Shorter Radar, satellites
Infrared Shorter Heat, remote controls
Visible Light Very short Vision
Ultraviolet Short Sterilization
X-rays Very short Medical imaging
Gamma Rays Shortest Cancer treatment

14. Frequency and Wavelength

In the electromagnetic spectrum, wavelength and frequency have an inverse relationship when the wave speed remains constant.

Higher Frequency → Shorter Wavelength

Lower Frequency → Longer Wavelength

15. Solved Numerical

Question:

An electromagnetic wave has a frequency of 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz. Find its wavelength in vacuum.

Given:

c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

f = 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz

Formula:

c = fλ

Rearranging:

λ = c ÷ f

Solution:

λ = (3 × 10⁸) ÷ (5 × 10¹⁴)

λ = 6 × 10⁻⁷ m

16. Important Points

  • Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves.
  • They can travel through vacuum.
  • They carry energy.
  • Speed in vacuum is 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
  • They are produced by changing electric and magnetic fields.
  • They include radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays.
  • They follow the relationship c = fλ.
  • Higher frequency means shorter wavelength when speed is constant.
