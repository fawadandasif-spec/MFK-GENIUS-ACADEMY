🔌 Electric Current

Federal Board Physics — Electricity Chapter

1. What is Electric Current?

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor.

In metallic conductors, electric current is produced mainly due to the movement of free electrons.

I = Q / t

Where:

  • I = electric current
  • Q = electric charge
  • t = time

2. SI Unit of Electric Current

The SI unit of electric current is the Ampere (A).

1 Ampere = 1 Coulomb / 1 Second

Therefore, a current of one ampere means that one coulomb of charge passes through a point in one second.

3. Direction of Electric Current

There are two ways to describe the direction of charge flow.

  • Conventional current: considered to flow from the positive terminal to the negative terminal of a source through the external circuit.
  • Electron flow: electrons in a metallic conductor move from the negative terminal toward the positive terminal.
Important: Conventional current direction is opposite to the direction of electron flow in a metal.

4. Relationship Between Current and Charge

From the formula for current:

I = Q / t

We can rearrange the equation to find charge:

Q = It

And to find time:

t = Q / I

5. What Determines Electric Current?

  • The amount of electric charge flowing through a conductor.
  • The time taken by the charge to flow.
  • The potential difference applied across the conductor.
  • The resistance of the conductor.
For a given conductor, increasing the potential difference generally increases the current.

6. Electric Current in Conductors

In metallic conductors, many electrons are free to move. When a potential difference is applied, these electrons acquire a net drift motion, producing electric current.

7. Measurement of Electric Current

Electric current is measured using an instrument called an ammeter.

  • Ammeter measures electric current.
  • Its SI unit is ampere (A).
  • An ammeter is connected in series with the circuit.
Remember: Ammeter → measures current → connected in series.

8. Solved Numerical — Finding Current

Question:
A charge of 20 C passes through a conductor in 5 seconds. Find the current.

Given:

  • Q = 20 C
  • t = 5 s

Formula:

I = Q / t

Solution:

I = 20 / 5

I = 4 A

9. Solved Numerical — Finding Charge

Question:
A current of 3 A flows through a conductor for 10 seconds. Find the charge transferred.

Given:

  • I = 3 A
  • t = 10 s

Formula:

Q = It

Solution:

Q = 3 × 10

Q = 30 C

10. Current and Charge Comparison

⭐ Important Points

  • Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge.
  • The symbol of current is I.
  • The SI unit of current is ampere (A).
  • Formula: I = Q/t.
  • Charge can be calculated using Q = It.
  • An ammeter is used to measure current.
  • An ammeter is connected in series.
  • Conventional current flows from positive to negative.
  • Electron flow in metals is opposite to conventional current.
