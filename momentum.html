Momentum

Learn momentum, impulse, conservation of momentum, collisions and their applications.

1. What is Momentum?

Momentum is the quantity of motion possessed by a moving object. It depends on the mass and velocity of the object.

p = mv

Where:
p = momentum
m = mass
v = velocity

The SI unit of momentum is kg m/s.

2. Momentum is a Vector Quantity

Momentum is a vector quantity because it has both magnitude and direction.

The direction of momentum is the same as the direction of velocity.

3. Factors Affecting Momentum

  • Momentum increases when mass increases.
  • Momentum increases when velocity increases.
  • Momentum becomes zero when the object is at rest.
p = mv

4. Impulse

Impulse is the product of force and the time for which the force acts on an object.

Impulse = F × Δt

Impulse is also equal to the change in momentum.

Impulse = Δp

5. Force and Change in Momentum

Force can be related to the rate of change of momentum.

F = Δp ÷ Δt

This relationship is another form of Newton's second law.

6. Law of Conservation of Momentum

The total momentum of an isolated system remains constant if no external force acts on the system.

Total Momentum Before = Total Momentum After

Example

When two objects collide, momentum can transfer from one object to another, but the total momentum of the system remains conserved when external forces are negligible.

7. Collisions

A collision occurs when two or more objects interact with each other for a short period of time.

  • Elastic collision: Kinetic energy is conserved.
  • Inelastic collision: Kinetic energy is not conserved.

Momentum is conserved in both types of collisions when the system is isolated.

8. Applications of Momentum

  • Airbags reduce the effect of collision forces.
  • Seat belts help protect passengers during sudden stops.
  • Sports players use follow-through to change momentum.
  • Rocket propulsion involves momentum conservation.
  • Hammers transfer momentum to nails.

9. Numerical Example

Question

A body of mass 4 kg moves with a velocity of 5 m/s. Find its momentum.

Given:

m = 4 kg
v = 5 m/s

Formula:

p = mv

Calculation:

p = 4 × 5

p = 20 kg m/s

Answer: Momentum = 20 kg m/s

10. Impulse Numerical

Question

A force of 20 N acts on an object for 3 seconds. Find the impulse.

Formula:

Impulse = F × Δt

Calculation:

Impulse = 20 × 3

Impulse = 60 N s

Answer: Impulse = 60 N s

11. Quick Revision

  • Momentum = mass × velocity.
  • p = mv.
  • Momentum is a vector quantity.
  • SI unit of momentum = kg m/s.
  • Impulse = Force × Time.
  • Impulse = Change in momentum.
  • F = Δp ÷ Δt.
  • Total momentum is conserved in an isolated system.
  • Momentum is conserved in elastic and inelastic collisions.
